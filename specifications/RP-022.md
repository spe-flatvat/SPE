## 1. Background

During M18 endpoint evaluation testing, the existing
bond calculation was identified as a potential mismatch
with Japan's public government bond issuance framework.

M18 is suspended pending this audit.

M1 through M17 completion records remain unchanged.

## 2. Identified Issue

Current implementation:

    bonds = max(expenditure - tax_revenue, 0)

This represents an approximation of the fiscal funding
gap, not necessarily total government bond issuance.

The implementation does not explicitly distinguish:

- New financing bonds
- Refunding bonds
- Other government bond issuance categories
- Bond redemptions
- Outstanding government debt
- Non-tax government revenue

## 3. Audit Objective

Verify consistency between Japan's publicly documented
government bond issuance framework and the SPE fiscal
model specifications and implementation.

Determine the definitions and required calculations for:

- Fiscal balance
- New financing bond issuance
- Total government bond issuance
- Government bond redemptions
- Outstanding government debt

## 4. Audit Scope

- Japanese public laws and Ministry of Finance materials
- Simulation Specification v6 and related documents
- M10 result generation
- M12 result validation
- M18 endpoint evaluation
- Downstream compatibility and regression requirements

## 5. Completion Criteria

1. Produce a mapping between public fiscal rules
   and existing SPE specifications.
2. Identify missing variables, initial conditions,
   and calculation formulas.
3. Assess impacts on existing milestones and outputs.
4. Determine whether the fiscal model should be
   retained, corrected, or extended.
5. Define required regression and validation tests.

## 6. Constraints

- Do not modify existing engine behavior before
  the audit decision is approved.
- Do not treat the current bonds indicator as
  verified total bond issuance.
- Preserve M1-M17 completion history.
- Keep M18 suspended until the audit resolution.

## 8. Public Framework Audit (RP-022-03A)

Audit Date: 2026-10-17

Status: Preliminary Findings Recorded

### 8.1 Scope

This audit compares the SPE fiscal model against
Japan's publicly documented government bond framework.

The following concepts must be distinguished:

- Fiscal funding gap
- New financing bond issuance
- Refunding bond issuance
- Total government bond issuance
- Bond redemptions
- Outstanding government debt
- Primary balance

### 8.2 Existing SPE Specification

Simulation Specification v5 Section 6 defines:

    Bond Issuance(t) =
        max(Expenditure(t) - Tax Revenue(t), 0)

Simulation Specification v6 retains this formula.

M10 implements the formula.
M12 validates the same formula.
M18 consumes the resulting bonds value.

Therefore, the current implementation follows the
existing SPE specification.

The identified issue is a potential mismatch between
the SPE specification and Japan's fiscal framework,
rather than an isolated M10 implementation defect.

### 8.3 Public Framework Findings

Japan's government bond issuance framework
distinguishes multiple issuance categories.

New financing bonds and refunding bonds serve
different financing purposes.

Refunding bonds are used to finance redemption
of existing government bonds.

Total bond issuance is not equivalent to the
general-account tax-expenditure funding gap.

Government debt outstanding is a stock variable
and cannot be inferred from annual issuance alone.

Primary balance is a separate fiscal indicator
and is not identical to bond issuance.

These findings concern institutional definitions.
They do not yet approve any replacement SPE formula.

### 8.5 Provisional Model Requirements

Candidate relationships for later specification review:

    Funding Gap =
        Expenditure - Tax Revenue - Non-Tax Revenue

    Total Bond Issuance =
        New Financing Bonds
        + Refunding Bonds
        + Other Bond Issuance

    Debt Stock(t) =
        Debt Stock(t-1)
        + Issuance(t)
        - Redemption(t)
        + Adjustments(t)

    Primary Balance =
        Tax Revenue
        + Non-Tax Revenue
        - Primary Expenditure

These are preliminary model candidates, not
approved canonical SPE equations.

Accounting scope, definitions, and adjustments
must be established before adoption.

## 9. Detailed Institutional Audit (RP-022-04)

### 9.2 Institutional Findings

#### General Account and Debt Consolidation Fund

The General Account and the Government Debt
Consolidation Fund Special Account have different
accounting functions.

The Debt Consolidation Fund handles government
bond redemption and interest-related operations.

Transfers between government accounts must not
be double-counted when constructing an aggregated
government fiscal model.

#### Bond Issuance Categories

Japanese government bond issuance distinguishes:

- Construction bonds
- Special deficit-financing bonds
- Refunding bonds
- Fiscal Investment and Loan Program bonds
- Other legally designated bond categories

New financing bonds and refunding bonds must
not be treated as equivalent fiscal indicators.

#### Refunding and Redemption

Refunding bonds finance redemption of existing debt.

Refunding issuance and annual redemption amounts
need not be identical because of timing differences,
including advance refunding arrangements.

The statutory 1.6 percent transfer mechanism
must not be interpreted as an annual 1.6 percent
reduction of all outstanding government debt.

#### Primary Balance

Primary balance must be defined independently
from gross bond issuance.

Its calculation requires a distinction between
primary expenditure and debt-service expenditure.

### 9.3 Legal Framework Review

Relevant legal provisions identified:

- Public Finance Act, Article 4
- Act on Special Accounts, Article 38
- Act on Special Accounts, Article 40
- Act on Special Accounts, Article 42
- Act on Special Accounts, Article 46
- Act on Special Accounts, Article 47

Construction bond issuance and special
deficit-financing bond issuance have different
legal foundations.

The enacted and effective status of FY2026
special deficit-financing legislation remains
an explicit verification item.

This audit record does not assert that all
statutory details have been exhaustively verified.

### 9.4 Recommended SPE Accounting Architecture

Recommendation: two-layer fiscal architecture.

Layer 1: General Account / Policy Fiscal Layer

Candidate responsibilities:

- Tax revenue
- Non-tax revenue
- Primary expenditure
- Debt-service expenditure
- New financing bonds
- Primary balance

Layer 2: Debt Management / Stock-Flow Layer

Candidate responsibilities:

- Outstanding government debt
- Redemption schedule
- Refunding bonds
- Interest expenditure
- Annual debt-stock transitions

This architecture is a recommendation,
not an approved SPE specification.

FILP bonds and other special-purpose issuance
categories require an explicit scope decision.

### 10.2 Confirmed Existing Model Behavior

SimulationState carries tax_revenue and expenditure
as independent state variables.

The M5 provisional dry run initializes:
- tax_revenue = 80.0 trillion JPY
- expenditure = 108.0 trillion JPY

The M5 source explicitly describes expenditure
as inferred from 80 + 28.

The expenditure transition is:

expenditure(t) =
    expenditure(t-1) * (1 + expenditure_growth(t))

The existing bonds calculation is:

bonds(t) =
    max(expenditure(t) - tax_revenue(t), 0)

This formula is specified in SPE v5 and v6,
implemented in the simulation output,
and enforced by M12 validation.

### 10.3 Accounting Interpretation

The current bonds variable directly measures
the positive difference between modeled
expenditure and modeled tax revenue.

It must not be identified without qualification
as Japan's gross government bond issuance.

The accounting scope of expenditure is not
sufficiently defined in the inspected specification.

The inspected model does not separately represent:
- Non-tax general account revenue
- Policy expenditure and debt service
- New financing bonds and refunding bonds
- Debt redemption and outstanding debt stock
- A separately calculated primary balance

These are findings within the inspected scope,
not proof of absence throughout the repository.

The 2026 expenditure initialization reproduces
the assumed 28 trillion JPY difference by construction.
It does not independently validate bond issuance.

### 10.4 Downstream Dependencies

M9, M10, and M11 contain regression checks
coupling expenditure changes and bonds changes.

M12 explicitly validates the existing bonds formula.

M18 reads the final-year bonds value as
bond_issuance_endpoint.

SPE v6 also refers to avoidance of rapid
Primary Balance deterioration using
Bond Issuance divergence.

The current model does not independently calculate
the primary balance required for a strict
institutional interpretation of that constraint.

### 10.5 Preliminary Impact Assessment

A future fiscal accounting revision may require:
- Clarification of expenditure accounting scope
- Explicit definitions for fiscal balance indicators
- Revisions to M10 output calculations
- Revisions to M12 validation rules
- Review of M9-M11 regression assumptions
- Review of M18 evaluation metric semantics
- Revision of the canonical SPE specification
- Backward compatibility and regression testing

These are impact candidates, not approved changes.

## 11. Fiscal Architecture Options (RP-022-05B)

### 11.1 Design Objective

Select an accounting architecture suitable for
long-term comparison of SPE policy scenarios.

The architecture must distinguish fiscal flows
from government debt stock and refinancing flows.

No architecture is approved at this stage.

### 11.2 Architecture Options

Option A: Retain Simplified Model

- Preserve existing expenditure and bonds equations
- Interpret bonds as simplified financing gap
- Minimal implementation impact
- Insufficient institutional debt accounting

Option B: General Account Extension

- Separate tax and non-tax revenue
- Separate primary expenditure and debt service
- Calculate primary balance independently
- Distinguish new financing bonds
- Limited treatment of refinancing and debt stock

Option C: Two-Layer Fiscal Architecture

Layer 1: General Account / Policy Fiscal Layer

- Tax revenue
- Non-tax revenue
- Primary expenditure
- Debt-service expenditure
- General Account financing requirements
- New financing bonds
- Primary balance

Layer 2: Debt Management / Stock-Flow Layer

- Opening debt stock
- New financing bond issuance
- Refunding bond issuance
- Scheduled redemption
- Interest expenditure
- Closing debt stock
- Maturity structure

Inter-layer transfers and flows must be
reconciled without double counting.

### 11.3 Preliminary Comparison

| Criterion | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Institutional fidelity | Low | Medium | High |
| Independent primary balance | No | Yes | Yes |
| Debt-stock dynamics | No | Limited | Yes |
| Refunding and redemption | No | Limited | Yes |
| Interest-rate transmission | No | Limited | Yes |
| Implementation complexity | Low | Medium | High |
| Long-term SPE suitability | Limited | Medium | High |

These ratings are preliminary design assessments,
not empirical validation results.

### 11.4 Preliminary Recommendation

Option C is the preferred design candidate.

Implementation should initially use annual
aggregate accounting with maturity buckets,
rather than full security-level bond simulation.

The accounting boundary must be explicitly defined.

FILP bonds, special-purpose bonds, and advance
refunding remain separate scope decisions.

The existing bonds variable must not silently
change meaning.

Backward compatibility and historical result
comparability require explicit treatment.

### 11.5 Proposed Accounting Principles

- Distinguish fiscal flows from debt stocks
- Distinguish new financing from refunding
- Calculate primary balance independently
- Avoid inter-account transfer double counting
- Preserve stock-flow accounting identities
- Avoid immediate repricing of all outstanding debt
- Use common accounting rules across scenarios
- Reconcile initial fiscal values before calibration

### 11.6 Pending Decisions

- Formal selection of architecture
- General Account versus consolidated coverage
- Debt-stock accounting boundary
- Treatment of FILP and special-purpose issuance
- Advance refunding treatment
- Maturity-bucket resolution
- Interest-rate and coupon modeling
- 2026 fiscal baseline reconciliation
- Legacy bonds compatibility strategy
- M10-M18 migration and validation plan

## 13. Fiscal Equations and Reconciliation Candidates (RP-022-05B)

### 13.1 Status and Scope

This section records provisional fiscal equations.

Option C remains recommended but unapproved.

These equations are not canonical SPE rules.

The accounting perimeter, fiscal-year timing,
and initial values require formal decisions.

### 13.2 Primary Balance Candidate

For a defined accounting perimeter:

PB(t) = PR(t) - PE(t)

Where:

- PB(t): primary balance
- PR(t): primary revenue
- PE(t): primary expenditure

Primary revenue excludes debt issuance.

Primary expenditure excludes interest and
principal redemption, subject to the selected
accounting definition.

A negative PB represents a primary deficit.

The exact treatment of transfers, non-tax
revenue, and accounting adjustments is pending.

General Account PB and consolidated national/
local government PB must not be conflated.

### 13.3 General Account Financing Identity

For a consistently defined cash-flow boundary:

Uses(t) = Sources(t)

Candidate components:

Uses(t):
- Primary expenditure
- Interest expenditure
- Debt-service transfers where applicable
- Other cash uses

Sources(t):
- Tax revenue
- Non-tax revenue
- New financing bond proceeds
- Other financing resources
- Applicable cash-balance adjustments

This is a classification framework, not an
approved calculation formula.

New financing bond issuance must be derived
from a fully reconciled financing requirement.

It must not automatically be set equal to
the negative primary balance.

Debt-service transfers and underlying redemption
must not be counted twice.

### 13.4 Debt Stock-Flow Identity

For a consistently defined debt perimeter:

D(t) = D(t-1) + I(t) - R(t) + A(t)

Where:

- D(t): closing debt stock
- I(t): total issuance in the selected perimeter
- R(t): principal redemption in that perimeter
- A(t): other stock adjustments

Candidate issuance decomposition:

I(t) = NF(t) + RF(t) + OI(t)

Where:

- NF(t): new financing issuance
- RF(t): refunding issuance
- OI(t): other issuance in the selected perimeter

The decomposition requires mutually exclusive
issuance categories.

Refunding issuance and related redemption
must be represented as separate gross flows.

They may offset in net debt-stock movement
without disappearing from gross issuance.

### 13.5 Interest and Maturity Dynamics

Candidate requirements:

- Interest costs depend on outstanding cohorts.
- New issuance receives cohort-specific terms.
- Existing debt is not automatically repriced
  at the current-year market interest rate.
- Scheduled maturity determines principal due.
- Refunding and redemption are reconciled.
- Stock adjustments are explicitly recorded.

Coupon, maturity, rollover, and timing rules
remain open design decisions.

### 13.6 Legacy Compatibility

Existing result schemas:

- m10.1
- m16.1
- m17.1

Existing annual output field:

bonds = max(expenditure - tax_revenue, 0)

This field is a legacy positive-gap indicator.

It must not be relabeled as actual gross
Japanese government bond issuance.

M12 enforces the legacy identity.

M16 and M17 validators delegate base-field
validation to M12.

M11 currently requires m10.1 results.

M18 currently dispatches among m10.1,
m16.1, and m17.1, and uses the legacy bonds
field for its bond issuance endpoint.

M18 remains suspended.

Candidate migration strategy:

- Preserve legacy output semantics.
- Introduce a separately versioned fiscal schema.
- Add dedicated fiscal accounting validation.
- Define explicit adapters for comparisons.
- Review endpoint evaluation semantics.
- Preserve reproducibility of historical results.

This migration strategy is not yet approved.
